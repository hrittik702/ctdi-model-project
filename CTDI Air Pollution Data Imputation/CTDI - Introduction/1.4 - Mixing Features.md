## Ques. Why is mixing features useful?

Imagine the model sees:

```text
PM2.5 ↑
Humidity ↑
Wind speed ↓
```

Those variables might collectively provide useful information about the pollution state.

Instead of treating them as completely independent:

```text
PM2.5
PM10
NO2
Temperature
Humidity
```

the CNN can learn combinations such as:

```text
PM2.5 + PM10 + humidity
```

or

```text
NO2 + temperature + wind
```

The paper describes the 1×1 CNN as learning interactions between different types of data features.

So its job is basically:

> **Feature interaction / feature transformation**

Not:

> "Find relationships between Station A and Station B."

That second job belongs to the spatial Transformer.
 

## Then comes the Spatial Transformer

Now things get interesting.

Suppose we're looking at one 24-hour period:

```text
Station A ─┐
Station B ─┤
Station C ─┤ → Spatial Transformer
Station D ─┘
```

The spatial Transformer asks:

> **How are the different stations related to each other?**

For example:

```text
Station A ←→ Station B
Station A ←→ Station C
Station B ←→ Station D
```

Maybe Station A is close to Station B, so their pollution patterns are often related.

If Station A has a missing PM2.5 value:

```text
Station A → ?
Station B → 82
Station C → 79
Station D → 84
```

information from other stations can help.

The CTDI architecture therefore first treats the data as **station-based sequences** and applies the spatial Transformer.

## Then comes the Temporal Transformer

After spatial processing, CTDI rearranges/transposes the tensor.

Before:

```text
Station → Time → Features
```

After transposing:

```text
Time → Station → Features
```

Now the model looks at the data from the perspective of each hour.

For example:

```text
Hour 1  → Station A, B, C, D
Hour 2  → Station A, B, C, D
Hour 3  → Station A, B, C, D
...
Hour 24 → Station A, B, C, D
```

Then the temporal Transformer asks:

> **How are the different hours within this 24-hour period related?**

So:

```text
8 AM
 ↓
9 AM
 ↓
10 AM
 ↓
11 AM
 ↓
...
```

The Transformer can learn dependencies between different positions in that sequence using self-attention.

The paper explicitly describes this transpose from a station-based sequence to a time-based sequence before applying the temporal Transformer.

## Spatial vs Temporal Transformer

This is the most important distinction to understand.

### Spatial Transformer

Looks primarily at:

```text
Station A
Station B
Station C
Station D
...
```

Question:

> **Which stations contain useful information about each other?**

---

### Temporal Transformer

Looks primarily at:

```text
Hour 1
Hour 2
Hour 3
...
Hour 24
```

Question:

> **Which time points contain useful information about another time point?**

---

Therefore:

```text
             CTDI
              │
       ┌──────┴──────┐
       ↓             ↓
   Spatial        Temporal
 Transformer      Transformer
       │             │
       ↓             ↓
 spatial          temporal
 relationships    relationships
```

The clever part is that CTDI doesn't try to make one Transformer simultaneously understand everything. It explicitly separates the two dimensions.

The paper describes the S-T module as two Transformer units, one spatial and one temporal.
