
### Ques : What exactly is this "24-hour tensor"?

This is the foundation.

The paper represents the input roughly as:

```text
X ∈ R^(stations × time × features)
```

For example, imagine:

```text
10 stations
24 hours
13 features
```

Then:

```text
X.shape = (10, 24, 13)
```

Think of it as a 3D box:

```text
                 Features
              ┌───────────────┐
             /               /|
            /               / |
           ┌───────────────┐   |
           │               │   |
           │               │   |
Stations   │               │   │
           │               │  /
           │               │ /
           └───────────────┘
                 Time
```

Each individual value has three coordinates:

```text
(station, hour, feature)
```

For example:

```text
(Station 4, Hour 13, PM2.5)
```

might contain:

```text
85.4
```

while:

```text
(Station 4, Hour 14, PM2.5)
```

might be missing.

The CTDI paper specifically uses 24-hour windows and treats each station's 24-hour data as a sequence.

### Ques : Why 24 hours?

Because pollution isn't independent at every hour.

Suppose PM2.5 is:

```text
8 AM   → 72
9 AM   → 75
10 AM  → ?
11 AM  → 79
12 PM  → 82
```

The missing 10 AM value isn't random nonsense.

The model can potentially infer it from:

```text
9 AM + 11 AM + 12 PM
```

But there's another source of information.

Maybe at the **same time**, nearby stations have:

```text
Station A → ?
Station B → 76
Station C → 74
Station D → 78
```

So there are two kinds of relationships:

### Temporal

```text
Yesterday → Today → Tomorrow
       or
8 AM → 9 AM → 10 AM → 11 AM
```

### Spatial

```text
Station A
   ↕
Station B ←→ Station C
   ↕
Station D
```

CTDI is specifically designed to exploit **both**.
