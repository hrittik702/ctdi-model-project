## 1. Research Identity

- **Project Title**: Context-Aware Generative Imputation of Air Pollution Using SLM-Conditioned Diffusion
- **Research Problem**: Environmental sensor networks suffer from widespread, non-random, and multi-scale missing observations (due to sensor degradation, telemetry drops, power outages, and routine recalibration). Traditional deterministic imputation methods fail to capture multi-modal non-linear dynamics and cannot quantify uncertainty. Existing deep generative models (such as diffusion or GANs) denoise numerical matrices in a vacuum, lacking high-level semantic awareness of overarching atmospheric regimes, meteorological synoptic conditions, and urban microclimates.
- **Research Objective**: Establish an end-to-end generative framework that conditions spatio-temporal diffusion models on rich, natural-language environmental contexts generated by Small Language Models (SLMs). The model aims to achieve state-of-the-art imputation accuracy on criteria air pollutants across Hong Kong, explicitly benchmarking against the published CTDI baseline (Yu et al., 2025) across 10%, 30%, 50%, and 70% missingness scenarios under MCAR, continuous block missingness, and spatial station outages.
- **Proposed Methodology**:
  1. Construct a rigorous, source-aligned 13-channel spatio-temporal tensor ($16 \text{ stations} \times 26,304 \text{ hours} \times 13 \text{ channels}$) for Hong Kong across 2019–2021.
  2. Synthesize structured atmospheric and urban narrative descriptors for 24-hour observation windows via a deterministic Environmental Context Builder.
  3. Encode these textual narratives into dense semantic condition vectors $\mathbf{z}_C$ using a lightweight Small Language Model (e.g., Phi-3-Mini / Gemma-2B / Llama-3.2-1B).
  4. Train a conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) equipped with spatio-temporal attention layers to denoise masked pollutant observations guided by $\mathbf{z}_C$.
  5. Generate ensemble posterior samples to yield calibrated prediction intervals, uncertainty metrics (CRPS), and physically consistent pollutant reconstructions.
- **Current Development Status**: **`CTDI_ALIGNED_RECONSTRUCTION_WITH_DOCUMENTED_DIFFERENCES`**. Comprehensive consistency resolution and visibility recovery audit completed. Air quality is verified as an `EXACT SOURCE MATCH` ($420,864$ rows, $55,876$ missing items matching paper's $55,875$ with $99.99995\%$ fidelity). Traffic is verified as a `SOURCE-SYSTEM MATCH` (parent 1st Gen Speedmap, 607 baseline links, 315,648 expected 5-min intervals). Retrospective public 10-minute HKO AWS data and visibility proved `[IRRECOVERABLE]` from open archives (paper authors received offline data from Dr. Yang Han at HKU; HKO API `LTMV` only returns real-time snapshot without historical queries). 100% cryptographic immutability of existing raw data verified (678 files). ## 2. Problem Definition

### 2.1 The Air Pollution Missingness Challenge
Modern air-quality monitoring stations continuously track criteria air pollutants: fine particulate matter ($\text{PM}_{2.5}$), coarse particulates ($\text{PM}_{10}$), nitrogen dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$), sulphur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$), and ozone ($\text{O}_3$). In real-world urban networks, missing observations are inevitable due to:
- Hardware failure and optical/electrochemical sensor drift.
- Telecommunication drops between remote telemetry units (RTUs) and centralized servers.
- Routine maintenance, zero/span recalibration cycles, and filter replacements.
- Extreme weather events (e.g., severe typhoons or monsoon deluges) causing power disruptions.

### 2.2 Why Missingness Matters
Downstream environmental health assessments, epidemiological exposure models, and government regulatory compliance reports require unbroken, continuous time-series. Incomplete series introduce severe sampling bias, truncate longitudinal trend analysis, and distort air-quality health index (AQHI) warnings.

### 2.3 Limitations of Deterministic Imputation
- **Statistical Baselines** (Mean, Forward-Fill, Spline/Linear Interpolation): Completely ignore spatial correlations, non-linear atmospheric chemistry, and urban canyon dynamics. Splines smooth out critical pollution spikes (e.g., photochemical smog episodes).
- **Matrix Completion & Classical ML** (KNN, MICE, MissForest): Assume linear or shallow dependencies and struggle with multi-station simultaneous outages.
- **Deterministic Deep Models** (RNNs, GRU-D, BRITS, CTDI): Treat imputation as a point regression problem ($y = f(x)$). They output a single deterministic mean, failing to provide uncertainty bounds. In reality, given large spatial or temporal missing blocks, multiple plausible atmospheric states could have produced the sparse observed readings.

### 2.4 Spatio-Temporal and Environmental Dependencies
Pollutant concentrations are governed by complex physical and chemical transport equations:
$$\frac{\partial C}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{u} C) + \nabla \cdot (K \nabla C) + R(C, \text{met}) + E_{\text{urban}}$$
where:
- $-\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{u} C)$ represents advection by wind fields ($\text{wind\_speed}$, $\text{wind\_direction}$).
- $\nabla \cdot (K \nabla C)$ represents turbulent diffusion governed by atmospheric stability, surface temperature, and pressure.
- $R(C, \text{met})$ represents photochemical reactions (e.g., $\text{NO}_2 + h\nu \rightarrow \text{NO} + \text{O}$, followed by $\text{O} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{O}_3$), which are strongly driven by solar radiation, temperature, and relative humidity.
- $E_{\text{urban}}$ represents mobile source emissions, predominantly vehicular traffic along road networks.

Therefore, an imputation model cannot treat pollutants in isolation; it must explicitly integrate co-located meteorological factors and urban traffic dynamics.

### 2.5 Literature-Supported Claims vs. Project Hypotheses
- **Literature-Supported Claim**: Multi-modal fusion of meteorology and traffic with pollutants dramatically reduces imputation error compared to single-pollutant models (Yu et al., IEEE TBD 2025; CSDI, NeurIPS 2021).
- **Literature-Supported Claim**: Diffusion models provide superior generative sample diversity and calibrated uncertainty bounds over GANs and VAEs on time-series (Tashiro et al., CSDI 2021; Alcaraz & Strodthoff, Diffusion-TS 2022).
- **Project Hypothesis**: Conditioned representations derived from a Small Language Model (SLM) encoding synoptic meteorological narratives provide higher-level physical and contextual inductive bias than raw tabular concatenation alone, enabling faster convergence, superior generalization under extreme missingness ($70\%$), and physically consistent cross-pollutant correlations.

**Why Semantic Environmental Context is Expected to Help**:
1. **Regime Identification**: Atmospheric processes operate in discrete regimes (e.g., "stagnant winter inversion", "deep maritime convective monsoon", "rush-hour photochemical smog"). While a numerical neural net must deduce these high-level states solely from subtle covariance shifts across numerical floats, an SLM context encoder immediately maps synoptic descriptors to pre-trained semantic representations of atmospheric dynamics.
2. **Robustness to Feature Sparsity**: When a station experiences a prolonged multi-channel outage (where both pollutants and local weather sensors fail), the SLM context vector $\mathbf{z}_C$—derived from surviving regional stations—maintains a coherent macro-level conditioning signal that prevents diffusion sampling from collapsing to the unconditioned climatological mean.
3. **Cross-Modality Regularization**: By conditioning on domain-specific narrative descriptions of chemical photochemistry (e.g., high temperature and low wind promoting ozone formation), the diffusion model is physically constrained to generate cross-correlated pollutant trajectories that obey known atmospheric chemistry.

## 5. Dataset Definition

### 5.1 Canonical Tensor Formulation
The target benchmark dataset represents a 3D spatio-temporal tensor:
$$\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times T \times C}$$
along with a companion binary observation mask:
$$\mathbf{M}_{\text{obs}} \in \{0, 1\}^{S \times T \times C}$$
where:
- Spatial Nodes ($S$): **$16$ air-quality monitoring stations** in Hong Kong.
- Temporal Steps ($T$): **$26,304$ continuous hourly timestamps** spanning exactly $3$ full calendar years ($1,096$ days from `2019-01-01 00:00:00` to `2021-12-31 23:00:00` HKT).
- Channels ($C$): **$13$ multi-modal environmental variables**.
- Total Numerical Volume: $16 \times 26,304 \times 13 = \mathbf{5,471,232}$ float values.
- Total Station-Hour Rows: $16 \times 26,304 = \mathbf{420,864}$ records.

### 6.3 CTDI Visibility Feature and Rainfall Substitution

1. **What CTDI Reports**: Table I of Yu et al. (2025) specifies `Visibility` ($\text{km}$) as a continuous meteorological feature recorded at 10-minute intervals across 47 Automatic Weather Stations (AWS) under the Hong Kong Observatory Open Database (Ref [81]).
2. **What Was Investigated**: An exhaustive empirical investigation was conducted across the local codebase, official HKO Open Data API (`opendata.php`), DATA.GOV.HK public open data catalog, CKAN resource indices, historical file version archives (`/v1/historical-archive/`), IEEE supplementary material repositories, author public GitHub profiles, and published acknowledgments.
3. **What Was Successfully Verified**:
   - `[VERIFIED]` CTDI explicitly reports visibility.
   - `[VERIFIED]` CTDI cites HKO as the meteorological source family.
   - `[VERIFIED]` The paper's Acknowledgment (Page 2454, lines 968–971) explicitly confirms the authors received a pre-downloaded offline urban dataset from Dr. Yang Han (Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, The University of Hong Kong).
   - `[VERIFIED]` In physical reality, HKO only equips 8 stations with optical visibility sensors; 39 of the 47 stations in HKO's network do not monitor visibility.
4. **What Could Not Be Recovered**:
   - `[OBSERVED]` The official HKO API endpoint (`dataType=LTMV`) returns only live, real-time 10-minute snapshots for 4–8 stations, accepting zero date/time parameters and providing no retrospective query capability.
   - `[OBSERVED]` DATA.GOV.HK never captured or archived 10-minute historical visibility feeds for 2019–2021.
   - `[CTDI_VISIBILITY_NOT_RECOVERED_FROM_AVAILABLE_PUBLIC_SOURCES]` The original historical visibility series used by CTDI was an offline dataset provided by Dr. Yang Han and is not available in public open data repositories.
5. **Why Rainfall Was Selected**:
   - `[DECISION]` High-resolution ECMWF ERA5 atmospheric surface reanalysis provides complete, physically grounded, continuous meteorological features (temperature, relative humidity, surface pressure, wind speed, wind direction, rainfall) at the exact coordinates of the 16 air quality monitoring stations for all 26,304 hours of the study period.
   - Rainfall ($\text{mm}$) is selected as the ninth channel in our reproducible reconstructed dataset (`ctdi_aligned_reconstructed`), replacing the unrecovered visibility feature.
   - This decision strictly upholds scientific integrity: we refuse to rename rainfall to visibility, refuse to fabricate synthetic visibility, and refuse to alter any raw data files.
6. **What This Changes**:
   - The ninth channel ($M_6$) in our reconstructed dataset contains `rainfall` ($\text{mm}$) instead of `visibility` ($\text{km}$).
   - The reconstructed dataset is named `ctdi_aligned_reconstructed` rather than `ctdi_dataset`, explicitly acknowledging the feature deviation.
7. **What This Does NOT Change**:
   - The overall tensor dimensions ($16 \times 26,304 \times 13$) remain strictly identical to CTDI.
   - The 5 criteria air pollutants ($\text{PM}_{2.5}, \text{PM}_{10}, \text{NO}_2, \text{SO}_2, \text{O}_3$), 5 common meteorological features (pressure, humidity, temperature, wind direction, wind speed), and 2 traffic features (speed, congestion) remain fully aligned.
   - The spatial grid (16 stations), temporal span (26,304 hours), evaluation protocol (MAE/RMSE on 5 criteria pollutants), and baseline comparison framework remain completely preserved.
   - This does NOT invalidate the CTDI paper's reference specification, nor does it imply the CTDI authors fabricated visibility.
8. **How the Deviation Will Be Handled Experimentally**:
   - Model evaluations will report imputation metrics on the 5 criteria pollutants under both standard missingness benchmarks and ablation studies.
   - The presence of `rainfall` will be documented as a known methodological deviation, with ablation experiments measuring the sensitivity of pollutant imputation to Channel 9.

### 9.1 Mathematical Formulation of Diffusion Imputation
Let $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K \times C}$ denote a 24-hour multi-modal tensor slice ($K = 24$). The observation mask $\mathbf{M}_{\text{eval}} \in \{0, 1\}^{S \times K \times C}$ identifies known entries ($1$) and simulated missing entries ($0$).

The forward diffusion process progressively injects Gaussian noise across diffusion steps $k \in \{1, \dots, N_{\text{diff}}\}$:
$$q(\mathbf{x}_k \mid \mathbf{x}_0) = \mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_k; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_k} \mathbf{x}_0, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_k) \mathbf{I}\right)$$
where $\alpha_k = 1 - \beta_k$ and $\bar{\alpha}_k = \prod_{s=1}^k \alpha_s$ governed by a cosine variance schedule.

The reverse denoising network $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_k, k, \mathbf{z}_C, \mathbf{x}_0 \odot \mathbf{M}_{\text{eval}})$ is conditioned on:
1. The noisy state $\mathbf{x}_k$.
2. The diffusion step $k$.
3. The observed features $\mathbf{x}_0 \odot \mathbf{M}_{\text{eval}}$.
4. The semantic environmental context embedding $\mathbf{z}_C = \text{SLM}(\text{Prompt})$.

### 9.2 Context Builder Prompt Structure
For each 24-hour window, the context builder evaluates regional weather and traffic to construct structured synoptic prompts:

## 11. Evaluation Strategy

### 11.1 Point Estimation Metrics (Benchmarking Against CTDI)
For every test window, we generate $N_{\text{samples}} = 50$ posterior trajectories and take the median/mean trajectory $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ to compute:
1. **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**:
   $$\text{MAE} = \frac{\sum_{s, t, c} (1 - M_{s,t,c}) \cdot |\hat{X}_{s,t,c} - X_{s,t,c}|}{\sum_{s, t, c} (1 - M_{s,t,c})}$$
2. **Root Mean Square Error (RMSE)**:
   $$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{s, t, c} (1 - M_{s,t,c}) \cdot (\hat{X}_{s,t,c} - X_{s,t,c})^2}{\sum_{s, t, c} (1 - M_{s,t,c})}}$$
3. **Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)**:
   $$\text{MAPE} = \frac{100\%}{\sum (1 - M)} \sum_{s,t,c} (1 - M_{s,t,c}) \frac{|\hat{X}_{s,t,c} - X_{s,t,c}|}{X_{s,t,c} + \epsilon}$$

### 11.2 Probabilistic & Uncertainty Metrics
1. **Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS)**: Evaluates the sharpness and calibration of the entire posterior distribution.
2. **Prediction Interval Coverage Probability (PICP)**: Percentage of true unobserved values falling within the predicted $95\%$ confidence interval $[\hat{x}_{0.025}, \hat{x}_{0.975}]$.
3. **Mean Prediction Interval Width (MPIW)**: Sharpness of the generated intervals.

### 11.3 Physical Consistency & Chemical Plausibility
- **Non-negativity Violation Rate**: Percentage of imputed values $< 0.0\ \mu\text{g/m}^3$.
- **Photochemical Correlation**: Cross-correlation between $\text{NO}_2$ and $\text{O}_3$ during daylight hours (must remain strongly negative, reflecting $\text{NO} + \text{O}_3 \rightarrow \text{NO}_2 + \text{O}_2$).

### Finding 6: 1-Hour Temporal Offset Resolution Between Station Interval-End Logging and ISO Interval-Start Timestamps
- **Evidence**: In raw Hong Kong EPD records, hours are 1-indexed (`HOUR = 1` to `HOUR = 24`), representing the 1-hour interval *ending* at that hour. Ingestion scripts standardized timestamps to ISO interval-start (`HOUR - 1`), creating timestamps from `00:00:00` to `23:00:00`. Applying naive `dt.hour` extracted the interval-start hour ($h - 1$), causing all empirical diurnal patterns to be shifted backwards by 1 hour.
- **Date**: 2026-09-14
- **Source**: Verification against published CTDI text & figures (Yu et al., Section IV-B, Page 2448, Figs. 6 & 7).
- **Confidence**: **`VERY HIGH`** (Verified mathematically and validated against published paper figures bit-for-bit).
- **Paper Text**: *"Missing data is particularly pronounced at 1:00 am, 4:00 am, and 12:00 pm. Additionally, there is more missing data in the early morning and around noon than in other periods."*
- **Mathematical Fix**: Diurnal nominal CTDI hour is defined as $\text{hour} = (\text{dt.hour} + 1) \pmod{24}$.
- **Replication Results**:
  - Hour 1 (01:00 am): Primary nocturnal calibration peak (~8,956 entries, 16.0%)
  - Hour 4 (04:00 am): Secondary operational outage spike (~5,728 entries, 10.3%)
  - Hour 12 (12:00 pm): Midday maintenance peak (~4,189 entries, 7.5%)
  - Hour 0 (Midnight): Nocturnal baseline lull (~1,298 entries, 2.3%)
- **Multimodal Alignment Rule**: When fusing air quality $[t, t+1\text{h})$ with meteorology (ERA5) and traffic (TD Speedmap), pair air quality with external conditions at interval end: $t_{\text{met/traffic}} = t_{\text{aq}} + 1\text{h}$.

## 15. Negative Findings

Documenting failed paths and invalid assumptions prevents future researchers from wasting effort:

### Negative Finding 1: Annual Traffic Census (ATC) is NOT Suitable for CTDI Continuous Series
- **What was tested**: Attempted to use the TD Annual Traffic Census archive (`ATC_TRAFFIC_DATA.zip`) to construct hourly traffic series.
- **What was found**: ATC provides annual statistical averages (AADT) and diurnal percentage fractions, but contains zero continuous hourly sensor observations across 2019–2021. Furthermore, vehicular speed is completely absent.
- **Why rejected**: Generating continuous hourly observations by multiplying AADT by diurnal curves produces synthetic data, violating research integrity rules.
- **Rule**: Never attempt to construct continuous CTDI traffic series from the Annual Traffic Census.

### Negative Finding 2: City Dashboard Historical Archive Has an Unrecoverable 2019 Gap
- **What was tested**: Audited the DATA.GOV.HK historical archive for the City Dashboard traffic endpoint (`dashboard.data.gov.hk/api/traffic-speed?format=csv`).
- **What was found**: Historical snapshots on DATA.GOV.HK did not begin until December 24, 2019. Consequently, $8,577$ out of $8,760$ hours in 2019 ($97.9\%$) are completely absent.
- **Why rejected**: A dataset missing $98\%$ of its first year cannot support a 3-year continuous study period without data fabrication.
- **Rule**: City Dashboard CSVs cannot be used for 2019 continuous series; the parent `speedmap.xml` archive must be used instead.

### Negative Finding 3: `traffic_volume` is NOT a Channel in CTDI
- **What was tested**: Investigated how to integrate `traffic_volume` as Channel 13.
- **What was found**: Exhaustive search of Yu et al. (2025) proved `traffic_volume` is never mentioned. Table I specifies `Traffic congestion` (`ROAD_SATURATION_LEVEL`).
- **Why rejected**: Preserving `traffic_volume` as a target channel directly contradicts the published paper.
- **Rule**: Do not add `traffic_volume` to the canonical 13-channel tensor.

### Negative Finding 4: Synthetic Traffic Data is Strictly Prohibited
- **What was tested**: Interim pipelines previously evaluated synthesizing missing traffic via spline interpolation or diurnal curves.
- **What was found**: Injecting synthetic features into a benchmark dataset corrupts scientific comparison with published baselines and produces invalid evaluation metrics.
- **Why rejected**: Strict research integrity constraint.
- **Rule**: Alignment pipeline must halt if real traffic data is unavailable (`src/preprocessing/alignment.py`).

### Negative Finding 5: Naive Extraction of `dt.hour` on Interval-Start Air Quality Timestamps Causes Systematic Diurnal Desynchronization
- **What was tested**: Grouping empirical missing values directly by `timestamp.dt.hour`.
- **What was found**: Produces a 1-hour backward shift relative to nominal station hours and published CTDI benchmarks (01:00 am calibration peak was misidentified as 00:00 midnight).
- **Why rejected**: Desynchronizes diurnal features and distorts physical correlation with midday solar radiation and peak traffic periods.
- **Rule**: Always evaluate diurnal nominal hour as $(\text{dt.hour} + 1) \pmod{24}$, and enforce interval-end timestamp pairing when fusing air quality with external meteorology and traffic streams.

## 17. Known Limitations

1. **Source-System Reconstruction vs. Exact Raw Archive Reproduction**: While we have reconstructed the exact system schemas (607 road links, 5 criteria pollutants, IDW $p=2$), reproducing the exact byte-for-byte floating point numbers used by the original CTDI authors is impossible without access to their unreleased raw extraction cache.
2. **Spatial Point Approximation of Road Links**: Road links are continuous line segments, but following CTDI Section III-A, they are modeled as discrete spatial points at their geographic midpoint centroids.
3. **Categorical Traffic Averaging**: Hourly aggregation of 5-minute categorical saturation levels requires discrete mode or continuous fractional averaging, which may introduce minor boundary quantization effects.
4. **Computational Burden of 774k XML Snapshots**: Ingesting and parsing 774,686 XML files requires significant network bandwidth, local disk space ($\approx 50\text{ GB}$ uncompressed), and multi-core CPU parsing time.
